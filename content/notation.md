# Notation Contract

Every lesson in `content/` uses these and nothing else. Where a source paper differs, the lesson says so at the point of use and converts — it does not silently adopt the source's convention.

Fixed once here because the whole project is convention-sensitive: a sign slip in $\mathrm{ad}^*$ or in the curvature tensor propagates into every bound downstream.

## Groups and algebras

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $G$ | matrix Lie group, $\dim G = n$ |
| $\mathfrak g = T_eG$ | Lie algebra; $e$ the identity |
| $L_h(g)=hg$, $R_h(g)=gh$ | left / right translation |
| $X_\xi(g) = g\xi = (T_eL_g)\xi$ | left-invariant vector field generated by $\xi\in\mathfrak g$ |
| $[\xi,\eta] = \xi\eta-\eta\xi$ | bracket — the **matrix commutator** for matrix groups |
| $\mathrm{ad}_\xi\eta = [\xi,\eta]$ | adjoint action of the algebra |
| $\mathrm{Ad}_g\eta = g\eta g^{-1}$ | adjoint action of the group |
| $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle : \mathfrak g^*\times\mathfrak g\to\mathbb R$ | the canonical **metric-free** pairing |

**Coadjoint operators — plain duals, no sign flip:**

$$\langle \mathrm{ad}^*_\xi\mu,\eta\rangle = \langle\mu,\mathrm{ad}_\xi\eta\rangle,
\qquad
\langle \mathrm{Ad}^*_g\alpha,\eta\rangle = \langle\alpha,\mathrm{Ad}_g\eta\rangle .$$

This matches [[@leeGeometricInterpretationBrownian2025]]. Some authors define $\mathrm{ad}^*$ with a minus sign; if a source does, convert at the point of use and say so.

:::warning[Type discipline — $\mathrm{ad}^*$ vs $\widetilde{\mathrm{ad}}$]
$\mathrm{ad}^*_\xi$ maps $\mathfrak g^*\to\mathfrak g^*$. An expression like $\sum_i\mathrm{ad}^*_{e_i}e_i$ therefore does **not** type-check on its own — it is shorthand for $\sum_i\widetilde{\mathrm{ad}}_{e_i}e_i=\sum_i\mathbb I^{-1}\mathrm{ad}^*_{e_i}(\mathbb I e_i)$, which needs the metric.

The distinction is not pedantry, and [[04-unimodularity]] shows why. The unimodularity defect splits into

$$\chi=-\mathrm{tr}\,\mathrm{ad}\in\mathfrak g^*\quad\text{(metric-free)},\qquad J=\mathbb I^{-1}\chi\in\mathfrak g\quad\text{(metric-dependent)}.$$

So *whether* the drift vanishes is intrinsic; its *size* is not. Sources — including [[@leeGeometricInterpretationBrownian2025]] — write $\mathrm{ad}^*$ for both because they have already identified $\mathfrak g^*\cong\mathfrak g$ via the inner product. Lessons must not.
:::

## Metrics

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $\mathbb I : \mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g^*$ | inertia operator, symmetric positive-definite |
| $\langle\xi,\eta\rangle_{\mathbb I} = \langle\mathbb I\xi,\eta\rangle$ | the inner product on $\mathfrak g$ it induces |
| $\widetilde{\mathrm{ad}}_\xi = \mathbb I^{-1}\mathrm{ad}^*_\xi\mathbb I : \mathfrak g\to\mathfrak g$ | **metric adjoint** |
| $\mathbb G$ | the left-invariant metric on $G$: $\mathbb G(u,v)|_g = \langle g^{-1}u,\,g^{-1}v\rangle_{\mathbb I}$ |
| $E_i(g) = g e_i$ | left-translated orthonormal frame, $\{e_i\}$ orthonormal in $\mathfrak g$ |
| $\mathbb G^\flat,\ \mathbb G^\sharp$ | index lowering / raising |

**Bi-invariant** means $\mathrm{Ad}$-invariant $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb I}$; exists on compact $G$, not on $SE(3)$.

## Connection and curvature

$\nabla$ is Levi-Civita for $\mathbb G$ unless explicitly said otherwise. When a flat left-invariant connection is meant, write $\nabla^-$ and say so — its torsion is $T(\eta_1,\eta_2)=-[\eta_1,\eta_2]$, and conflating the two splittings is the single easiest way to get a wrong sign.

$$R(X,Y)Z = \nabla_X\nabla_Y Z - \nabla_Y\nabla_X Z - \nabla_{[X,Y]}Z$$

$$\mathrm{Sec}(u,v) = \frac{\langle R(u,v)v,\,u\rangle}{\|u\|^2\|v\|^2-\langle u,v\rangle^2}$$

With this convention the round sphere has $\mathrm{Sec}>0$, and a bi-invariant metric gives $\mathrm{Sec}(X,Y)=\tfrac14\|[X,Y]\|^2\ge0$ for orthonormal $X,Y$.

$\mathrm{grad}\,\psi=\mathbb G^\sharp d\psi$; $\mathrm{Hess}\,\psi(u,w)=\langle u,\nabla_w\,\mathrm{grad}\,\psi\rangle$; $\mathrm{Hess}^\sharp\psi=\nabla\,\mathrm{grad}\,\psi$.

## Mechanics

:::warning[Three overloaded symbols — declare which you mean]
**$\Delta$.** Laplace–Beltrami everywhere, and Brownian motion is the diffusion with generator $\tfrac12\Delta$ — that meaning is load-bearing in Phases 4–5 and wins by default. [[12-double-tangent-bundle]] alone uses $\Delta$ for the **Liouville (Euler, dilation) field** on $TM$, following the standard second-order-systems literature, and declares the override at the top of that file. Do not use $\Delta$ for the Liouville field anywhere else; if a later lesson needs it, write $\mathcal E$.


**$\pi$.** Reserved below for spatial momentum, but $\pi:T^*Q\to Q$ (or $TM\to M$) is unavoidable as the bundle projection — it appears in $\theta_0$, in $\iota_{\mathrm{ver}(\beta)}\omega_0=-\pi^*\beta$, and throughout [[12-double-tangent-bundle]]. **Bundle projection wins in any lesson about bundles**; write spatial momentum as $\pi_{\mathrm{sp}}$ there, or avoid it. Declare the choice at first use.

**$n$.** Means $\dim G$ (equivalently $\dim\mathfrak g$, $\dim Q$) — the *configuration* dimension. The state manifold $T^*Q$ has dimension $N=2n$. Bounds quoted from sources that write $n$ for "dimension of the manifold the vector field lives on" must be converted: [[@simpson-porcoContractionTheoryRiemannian2014]]'s divergence bound is $\mathrm{div}_GX\le-N\lambda$ with $N=2n$ on $T^*Q$. In [[28-intrinsic-amgf]] and the other AMGF lessons $n$ is the dimension the sphere average runs over, so it is $N$ there, not $\dim G$ — say so explicitly.
:::

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $\xi = g^{-1}\dot g\in\mathfrak g$ | **body** (left-trivialized) velocity |
| $\mu = \mathbb I\xi\in\mathfrak g^*$ | body momentum |
| $\pi = \mathrm{Ad}^*_{g^{-1}}\mu$ | spatial momentum (see the warning above) |
| $\dot g = g\xi$ | reconstruction equation |
| $\dot\mu = \mathrm{ad}^*_\xi\mu + f$ | forced Euler–Poincaré, $f\in\mathfrak g^*$ |

**Left (body) trivialization throughout.** A right-invariant reduction flips the sign of the $\mathrm{ad}^*_\xi\mu$ term; if a source uses it, convert.

## $SO(3)$ and $SE(3)$

$\hat{\cdot}:\mathbb R^3\to\mathfrak{so}(3)$ with $\hat a\,b = a\times b$; inverse $(\cdot)^\vee$.

$$\langle\eta,\zeta\rangle_{\mathfrak{so}(3)} = \tfrac12\mathrm{tr}(\eta^\top\zeta) = (\eta^\vee)^\top(\zeta^\vee)$$

The $\tfrac12$ is not cosmetic — [[@leeGeometricInterpretationBrownian2025]]'s mean-curvature result $H=-2R$ is locked to it.

$\mathbb J = \mathbb J^\top\succ0$ is the inertia matrix, $\Omega\in\mathbb R^3$ the body angular velocity ($\hat\Omega = R^\top\dot R$), $\Pi=\mathbb J\Omega$ the body angular momentum. Euler: $\mathbb J\dot\Omega = \mathbb J\Omega\times\Omega+\tau$.

Use $SO(3)$ when bi-invariance is wanted, $SE(3)$ when its failure is the point.

## Exp and log — always say which

| Notation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $\exp_G:\mathfrak g\to G$, $\log_G$ | **Lie** exponential — flow of a left-invariant field; the matrix exponential for matrix groups |
| $\exp_p:T_pM\to M$, $\log_p$ | **Riemannian** exponential at $p$ — geodesics of $\nabla$ |

They agree iff $\nabla_\xi\xi=0$ for all $\xi$, i.e. for bi-invariant metrics. Never write a bare $\exp$ where both are in play. See [[riemannian-geometry]].

## Stochastics

$dW$ Itô, $\circ\,dW$ Stratonovich. $\mathcal L$ or $A$ is the generator. $\Delta$ is Laplace–Beltrami, and Brownian motion is the diffusion with generator $\tfrac12\Delta$.

$\mathrm{II}$ is the second fundamental form (ambient minus intrinsic) and $H=\sum_i\mathrm{II}(E_i,E_i)$ the mean curvature vector — **no $1/n$ factor**, differing from do Carmo.
